Ranked hints of bloat: where to look, not what to delete.

Kinds, in the order worth working through:
  duplicate          the same code more than once: an identical (or, for
                     long bodies, same-shape: names or literals differ;
                     `identical` says which) body and the same written
                     signature (parameter and return types, receiver,
                     generics and where clause, qualifiers; for methods
                     the impl's Self type, generics and trait; names
                     ignored). A body counts by its own logic, not its
                     text: --min-dup lines after the signature, and at
                     least 1 piece of logic (an operator, a branch or
                     loop, a closure, a call or macro given arguments;
                     constructors like `Self(x)` don't count), so bodies
                     that only forward to `self` or to another fn never
                     count. Identical free fns and inherent methods of
                     impl code count however short. Each group is reported
                     once with every copy and the facts about it: per copy
                     `crate`, `test`, `cfgs`, `signature`, `class` (its
                     signature among the group's); per group `crates`,
                     `depends` ([a, b]: a depends on b directly, per
                     Cargo.toml), `rule`, `hint`. `tier`: `finding` (worth
                     acting on; identical copies under different cfgs
                     too) or `note` (listed after findings, not marked in
                     the editor, counted in `duplicate_notes`): equal
                     bodies under different signatures (`differs` names
                     the parts: an owned/borrowed pair, typestates, one
                     body on two types) or same-shape cfg variants of one
                     crate. Hints are advice, not claims.
                     Limits: names aren't resolved (same-named items in
                     different files or crates, like consts or imported
                     types, count as the same); signatures compare as
                     written: lifetimes ignored, generic parameter names
                     as written (`<T>` vs `<U>`, `<T: X>` vs `where T: X`,
                     predicate order all differ), `Self` differs from the
                     concrete type, an alias from its type; bodies that
                     differ only in literals are data, never duplicates;
                     whether copies can call each other (visibility,
                     re-exports, types) isn't checked.
  unused             fns/types/traits whose name is mentioned nowhere
                     outside tests (incl. doctests), or for a fn only in
                     its own body. Mentions: identifiers in code,
                     attribute arguments and their strings, any macro's
                     tokens, include!d files, build-script strings; not
                     comments or other strings. Name-based, so verify: after
                     removing, run `cargo check --workspace --all-targets
                     --all-features` (or the project's usual feature sets)
                     and `cargo test --doc`, then re-run bloaty (removals can
                     expose more). Code only built under cfg(loom)/fuzzing
                     counts as a use. `uncertain: true` marks crates with
                     code bloaty can't see.
  single-impl-trait  traits with at most one impl (derives and impls in other
                     workspace crates count; traits with blanket impls and
                     extension traits on foreign types are exempt; no claim
                     for a trait named in a macro other than the
                     well-known expression macros, or whose name another
                     workspace trait shares). Fine as a mocking or plugin
                     boundary; otherwise inline.
  long-fn            more than --long-fn own code lines (nested items excluded),
                     as the smell score counts them: a table's literal, a
                     dispatch's small arms and a fn allowing
                     clippy::too_many_lines (below 80 lines) are excused
                     (counted in `excused`).
  test-heavy         files whose inline tests are >3x their code (and >= 200):
                     look for repeated setup to share and cases to merge
                     into table-driven tests.
  parametrize        tests of one scope (file and inline mod), with the
                     same attributes (`should_panic`, `ignore`,
                     `tokio::test(..)` as written) and signature and
                     distinct names (a name written twice, as in
                     cfg-exclusive mods, is variants of which at most one
                     compiles), whose bodies are identical except for
                     literals, and some literal differs: one test over a
                     table of those literals (or a helper taking them)
                     could replace them. Rows of one table have, per
                     literal position, one kind (string, byte string, C
                     string, char, byte, integer or float, a number's
                     suffix as written: `'a'` and `"a"`, `1u8` and `1i64`
                     can't share a column; raw and plain strings can) and
                     equal text where the literal isn't data: a tuple
                     index (`p.0`), an inline snapshot (`@"…"`), or
                     anything inside a macro other than the format!/
                     assert!/vec!-like and log/tracing macros read as
                     expressions (that macro's input: `include_str!`,
                     `quote!`, insta's and snapbox's snapshot macros);
                     doc comments in a body aren't literals. At
                     least 3 tests at any size, or a pair with --min-dup
                     lines after the signature in each. A test's lines are
                     those after its signature on which a token starts
                     (lines wholly inside a multi-line string don't count;
                     nested items do, as their literals do). Facts:
                     `tests` (each with its `lines` and `identical`: all
                     its literals are another test's), `literals` {differ,
                     of}: literal positions that differ, of all. Tests
                     with params (rstest, test_case) aren't tests as
                     written. Limits: tests in item macros whose body
                     parses as items are read as written, other
                     macro-written tests aren't seen; `true`/`false` are
                     names, not literals, so tests differing only in
                     booleans don't group; tests in different files or
                     mods never group; a literal that isn't data in ways
                     syntax can't tell still counts (an expression macro's
                     format string, an array length).
  pass-through       (only with --kind pass-through) private-ish fns whose
                     body is one call passing their inputs to another
                     workspace fn (no claim when several workspace fns
                     share the callee's name). Mostly a judgement call.

Within a kind, impl code ranks before test code and private before pub;
duplicate notes come last. `category` says impl or test (a duplicate is
impl if any copy is). `lines` counts code lines: the item's own (plus its
impl blocks for unused types), the largest copy's lines after its
signature (at least 1) for duplicates, test lines for test-heavy, the
tests' lines (as above) for parametrize.
`lines_saved` is at most what keeping one copy saves.

JSON per finding: `path` (absolute) and `file` (relative to `root`), `line`
(the name, to jump to), `start_line`..=`end_line` (the whole item with docs
and attributes, to delete), `crate`, and for some kinds
`impls`/`copies`/`tests` with the same fields (`copies[0]` and `tests[0]`
are the finding itself). `totals` per kind say what --top cut.

`pub` items of publishable libraries (anything not `publish = false`) are
left out of unused, single-impl-trait and pass-through, since code outside
the repository may use them: `hidden_pub` counts them. Mark internal crates
`publish = false` (or `publish.workspace = true` with the workspace's
`publish = false`) to have them checked, or pass --include-pub.

The JSON report also has `root`, `thresholds`, `test_features`, `totals` and
`shown` per kind (what --top kept; duplicate notes apart, in
`duplicate_notes` {total, shown}), and per criterion how many groups of
copies with equal text weren't duplicates after all (`excluded_duplicates`:
`substance`), and `excused` {kind: {excuse: n}}: items left out because
the smell score excuses them (long_fn: `table`, `dispatch`, `allowed`).

Limits: bloaty is syntactic. Names aren't resolved (same-named items count
as the same) and macros aren't expanded (only format!/vec!/assert!-like
std and log/tracing macros are read as expressions; other macros' words
are just mentions). Where a claim would need either, none is made:
`unclaimed` {kind: {reason: n}} counts them, reason `same_name` (another
definition shares the name the claim matches by) or `named_in_macro` (a
macro may implement the trait, or may declare the file: such files are
test or not by path only). Cfgs written through macros (cfg_rt!) aren't
seen, so copies under them read as plain duplicates. rust-analyzer is the place for precision.
